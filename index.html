Christmas Tree Decorating Stand — Build Plan

A height-adjustable, rotating stand that holds one section at a time of your 9 ft Costway tree (3,594 tips, unlit) at chest height — so you string the lights standing on the floor, spin the section instead of walking around it, plug the lights into the stand to check them, then lift the finished section out and stack it on the permanent display stand. No ladder, no bending over a section on the floor.

§1How it works

Four ideas do all the work. Everything else in this plan is just plywood, two steel tubes, and hardware holding them together.

01

Telescoping post

A 1¼ in tube slides inside a 1½ in tube. A ⅜ in carriage bolt through the pre-punched holes pins the height — platform from 31 to 42 in off the floor, in 1 in steps.

02

Rotating receiver

A 6 in lazy Susan bearing (rated 400 lb) sits between the post cap and the platform. The tree pole drops into a 6 in socket on the platform and snugs with a wing-nut clamp bolt — and lifts straight back out.

03

Power on the stand

A metal-case power strip is zip-tied to the outer (non-rotating) post, fed through a GFCI adapter at the wall. Plug each section's lights in and test them before the section ever leaves the stand.

04

Wide, braced base

A 24 × 24 in plywood base with four gussets tight against the post and anti-skid pads underneath. The post passes through the base and is pinned below it, so it can't pull out or rock loose.

The two steel tubes are $89 of that total — that's the price of the no-drilling convenience, since the pin holes come pre-punched. Plain (non-perforated) square tube plus drilling the holes yourself would save roughly $20–$30 if you want to cut cost.

⚠ Read this before you buy the tubes — the pin-hole catch

The 1½ in tube's punched holes are 7/16 in diameter (per the manufacturer), so the ⅜ in height pin passes through it easily. But the 1¼ in tube's punched holes come smaller — one buyer reported they are smaller than ⅜ in — so the pin won't fit the inner tube as it comes off the shelf.

The fix is one hole: enlarge the inner tube's single pin hole — the one 4 in from its bottom end — to ⅜ in with a step bit before assembly, and test-fit the ⅜ in pin through both tubes (Build Step 5). Also note the ⅜ in carriage bolt's square neck stops at the outer tube's wall — that is fine; the washer and nut on the far end are what hold it.

§4Build steps

Common tools only: drill with a step bit, jigsaw, hacksaw (or reciprocating saw with a metal blade), file, wrench, screwdriver, tape measure. Cut list first — everything wood comes off the one panel.

Cut list — plywood (from the one 2 × 4 ft panel)

  • Base — 24 × 24 in ×1
  • Receiver platform — 8 × 8 in ×1
  • Cap plate — 6 × 6 in ×1
  • Gussets — triangles, 4 in legs ×4
  • Brace blocks — 2 × 3 in ×2
  • Plug — 1 × 3 in ×1 (goes inside the inner tube top)

Cut list — steel tube

  • From the 1½ in tube: outer post 18 in, socket 6 in (12 in spare)
  • From the 1¼ in tube: inner post 30 in (6 in spare)
  • File every cut edge smooth before assembly
  • Socket fit check: inside ≈1.35 in — takes tree poles up to 1¼ in dia.; your Costway pole is ≈1 in

  1. Cut the plywood

    From the one panel, cut: base 24×24, platform 8×8, cap 6×6, four gusset triangles with 4 in legs, two brace blocks 2×3, and one plug 1×3 — all inches.

  2. Cut the tubes

    With a hacksaw (or reciprocating saw with a metal blade): from the 1½ in tube, an 18 in outer post and a 6 in socket; from the 1¼ in tube, a 30 in inner post. File the cut edges smooth.

  3. Base — cut the center hole, add the pads

    Mark a 1½ in square hole in the center of the 24×24 base. Drill a ⅜ in starter hole at one corner, cut the square with a jigsaw, and test-fit the outer tube — file the edges until it slides in snug. Stick the four anti-skid pads ⑭ under the base corners.

  4. Mount the outer post

    Slide the 18 in outer tube through the base hole so its top sits 18 in above the floor. Pin it below the base with a ⅜ in carriage bolt (stop pin ⑬) through the tube's lowest holes — washer + nut. Screw the four gussets ⑫ to the base tight against the tube's four faces, two #8 × 1½ in screws each.

  5. Inner post prep — enlarge the pin hole first

    Enlarge the pin hole 4 in from the bottom end to ⅜ in with a step bit (the 1¼ in tube's punched holes come smaller — see the warning in §3) and test-fit the pin through both tubes. Then tap the 1×3 wood plug into the top of the inner tube so it's flush with the top, and drive one #8 × 1½ in screw through the tube wall into the plug, using a punched hole as the pilot.

  6. Cap and bearing

    Screw the 6×6 cap down onto the plug with two #8 × 1½ in screws from above, about 2 in apart along the plug. Screw the lazy Susan's bottom plate to the cap with four #8 × ¾ in screws through the bearing's corner holes.

  7. Platform and socket

    Cut a 1½ in square hole in the center of the 8×8 platform (same method as the base). Slide the 6 in socket through and pin it below the platform with a ⅜ in carriage bolt — washer + nut. Screw the two brace blocks to the platform tight against two opposite socket faces, from below, with #8 × 1½ in screws. Drill two ¼ in holes through the socket at 3 in above the platform for the clamp bolt. Screw the bearing's top plate to the platform with four #8 × ¾ in screws.

  8. Assemble and set the height pin

    Slide the inner post into the outer post. Set the height pin ④ through a pair of aligned holes — washer + nut, finger-tight plus a quarter turn. Don't crank it.

  9. Power — strip on the OUTER post only

    Zip-tie the GE strip vertically to the outer post at about 12 in above the floor — two ties through the strip's mounting slots, around the post. The strip lives on the outer post so it never rotates. Run its cord down the post with two more ties ⑮. Plug the strip's cord into the Husky GFCI adapter ⑪, and the adapter into the wall outlet.

  10. Test before the first section

    Set the platform at the lowest pin. Drop a tree section in, snug the clamp bolt, and spin it — it should turn freely by hand. Plug the lights into the strip, switch the strip on, and check the lights. Press the GFCI adapter's test button to confirm it trips, then reset it. Now you're ready to decorate.

Tools: drill + step bit · jigsaw · hacksaw or reciprocating saw with metal blade · metal file · wrench · screwdriver · tape measure · pencil.

§5How to use it — section by section

This is the yearly routine the stand was designed around, for your Costway's sections.

  1. Set the height

    Pull the pin, slide the inner post until the section's middle will sit at chest height, and re-insert the pin. Tall bottom section: lowest two or three pin positions. Short top section: raise it up.

  2. Drop in one section

    Lower the section's pole into the socket and snug the wing-nut clamp bolt ⑨ by hand.

  3. String the lights, spinning as you go

    Turn the section with one hand while you dress it with the other — no ladder, no walking around it, no dragging a section across the floor.

  4. Plug in and check

    Plug the lights into the strip ⑩, switch the strip on, and step back across the room to check the coverage before the section goes anywhere.

  5. Transfer to the permanent stand

    Unplug, loosen the clamp, lift the section straight out, and carry it to the permanent display stand and stack it. Plug its dangling light plug into the section below as you stack.

  6. Repeat — then store it flat

    Same routine for each remaining section. When the season's over, the stand stores flat against a wall.

§6Safety notes

Weight limit: 25 lb per tree section

The bearing is rated 400 lb, but the stand's stability governs — 25 lb is the limit. Before loading any section, check the height pin passes fully through both tubes and the nut is on.

Stability

Keep the platform at the lowest setting that works for the section. Never lean on the section or pull it sideways. Keep kids and pets from climbing it. Use it on a flat, hard floor — on thick carpet the pads can sink, so put a plywood sheet under it if it wobbles.

Electrical

UL-listed strip plus GFCI adapter, always. Keep the strip cord and light cords clear of the rotating platform — route them down the outer post only. Lights only on the strip, no heaters. Unplug before lifting a section out. Inspect cords each season. Indoor use only, keep away from water.

Built for a Costway 9 ft unlit artificial tree (3,594 tips) — sections ≈2½–3½ ft tall, center pole ≈1–1¼ in dia., 10–20 lb each. Socket inside ≈1.35 in fits poles up to 1¼ in.
